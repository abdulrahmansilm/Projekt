<p>Ongoing managed IT in Germany typically costs between €25 and €80 per device per month. The range comes down almost entirely to two factors: which services are included and how quickly the provider has to respond. At Selim-IT, managed IT starts at €30 per device per month, including the essentials.</p>

<p>That only answers half the question, though, because "per device" is just one of several pricing models.</p>

## The three common pricing models
<h3>Per device</h3>

<p>The price is based on the number of devices under management: desktops, laptops, servers. The model is easy to calculate and works well for businesses where every employee has one device. The downside: if you have lots of rarely used devices, such as terminals in the warehouse, you pay for capacity that causes hardly any work.</p>

<h3>Per user</h3>

<p>You pay per person, regardless of whether they use a laptop, a tablet and a work phone. That pays off in businesses with many devices per head. It gets expensive when many people share a few devices.</p>

<h3>Time and materials</h3>

<p>Classic hourly billing, usually between €90 and €150 per hour. No base fee, but no predictability either, and nobody takes care of anything until you call. Sensible for one-off projects, rarely for ongoing support.</p>

<div class="keypoint">
<p><strong>A practical note:</strong> many providers combine models. A monthly base fee covers support and monitoring, while larger projects are billed separately by effort. What matters is that the line between the two is clearly drawn in writing.</p>
</div>

## What a solid base package should include
<p>These items are standard. If one of them is missing from a proposal, it's worth asking why:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Round-the-clock monitoring</strong>, automatic monitoring of devices and services with alerts when something looks off</li>
<li><strong>Troubleshooting</strong>: fixing problems within the agreed scope, without a separate invoice per incident</li>
<li><strong>Remote maintenance</strong>: clean-ups, optimization, configuration changes</li>
<li><strong>Ticket support</strong>, traceable logging and handling of requests</li>
<li><strong>Patch management</strong>, centrally managed updates for operating systems and software</li>
<li><strong>Antivirus</strong>, including the license and central management</li>
<li><strong>Ongoing documentation</strong>, an up-to-date overview of devices, accounts and configurations</li>
<li><strong>Monthly report</strong>: what was done, what's coming up, where action is needed</li>
</ul>

## What's usually billed separately
<p>These items generally aren't part of the base package and are listed separately. Ideally you should see them in the proposal from the start, so that total costs stay predictable:</p>

## The factor that drives the price most
<p>It isn't the number of devices, it's the guaranteed response time. A provider that has to respond within an hour keeps capacity on standby and charges for it. One that has 24 hours can bundle work and offer a lower price.</p>

<p>At Selim-IT, the regular response time is between 4 and 24 hours, and between 1 and 8 hours for emergencies, depending on the package. Regular support hours are 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.</p>

<p>For your own calculation, one simple question helps: what does a day of downtime cost us? If the answer is well above the monthly support fee, the faster response time is the cheaper choice.</p>

## Example: a business with ten workstations
<p>Ten workstations, one server, Microsoft 365, no special compliance requirements. At €39 per device per month, basic support for the workstations comes to €390 a month. The server is usually priced separately and higher, since it takes more work. On top of that come licenses and, if needed, backup storage.</p>

<p>For comparison: a part-time employee with reasonably solid IT skills costs many times that, with no cover during vacations, no specialist knowledge beyond their own focus and no monitoring outside working hours.</p>

## Where you can save without it hurting
<ul>
<li><strong>Annual instead of monthly contract:</strong> Selim-IT gives you a 10 to 20 percent discount for it. Other providers offer similar savings.</li>
<li><strong>Clean up licenses:</strong> former employees, duplicate licenses, plans that are bigger than needed. There's money here in almost every business.</li>
<li><strong>Consolidate devices:</strong> every managed device costs money. Old computers that nobody uses but that are still running belong out of the contract.</li>
<li><strong>Choose a realistic response time:</strong> not every business needs a one-hour emergency response. An honest assessment saves noticeably.</li>
</ul>

## How to recognize a good proposal
<p>Three things make a proposal comparable and reliable. First, a service description that states exactly what's included and what's billed separately. Second, binding response times in hours rather than vague promises. Third, a contract term that fits your needs: Selim-IT contracts can be cancelled monthly, and an annual contract is an optional extra with a discount.</p>
